1Four Highlights

🐠

Atauro Island reefs

An island off Dili, with reefs said to hold the world's highest average reef-fish species count. A mecca for diving and snorkelling; became its own municipality in 2022.

⛰️

Mt Ramelau (Tatamailau)

The highest peak at 2,963 m, with a pilgrimage trail and a popular summit sunrise. Nearby Maubisse is a cool hill retreat amid coffee country.

🏝️

Jaco Island (Lautém)

An uninhabited, sacred island at the eastern tip with crystal-clear water and white sand — part of Nino Konis Santana National Park, rich in nature and archaeology.

🐋

Whale & bird watching

The Ombai-Wetar Strait is a cetacean corridor (best ~Oct–Dec). Many endemic birds also make it prime birdwatching territory.

2Best Season & Climate

The north coast is relatively dry; the south and highlands are wetter. The dry season (roughly May–November) suits diving and trekking.

☀️ Dry season (~May–Nov)

Calmer seas with high visibility for diving; good for hiking and touring. Roads are relatively reliable.

🌧️ Wet season (~Dec–Apr)

More rain and squalls; mountain roads deteriorate. Greener scenery, fewer crowds — allow extra travel time.
Whale migration: cetaceans in the Ombai-Wetar Strait are best seen around Oct–Dec (please follow sustainable, low-impact viewing practices).

4Practical Information

  • Visa: Visa on Arrival US$30 / 30 days (extendable). Requirements can change — verify with immigration/embassy before travel.
  • Currency: the US dollar (centavo coins). Largely cash-based; ATMs/cards are limited. Carry small notes.
  • Language: official Tetun and Portuguese; some English and Indonesian are also understood.
  • Transport: left-hand traffic. Shared microlets in town; 4WDs/buses in the regions. Allow extra time on mountain roads.
  • Connectivity: a local SIM may require registration; coverage is centred on towns.
  • Power: access now reaches most of the country; carry a power bank for outages.
  • Time / codes: UTC+9 (same as Japan) / dialling +670 / ccTLD .tl.
Cameras & drones: bringing in large camera gear or drones may require customs declaration or prior permits. Check in advance.

5Health & Safety

  • Disease: beware mosquito-borne illness (malaria, dengue); use bite protection. TB burden is high. Consult a travel clinic on vaccinations/prophylaxis beforehand.
  • Medical care: specialist care is limited. Travel insurance (including medical evacuation) is strongly recommended. Bring your own medicines.
  • Water & hygiene: avoid tap water — use bottled or boiled water; sanitation is limited in the regions.
  • Road safety: beware unpaved mountain roads, rockfall and wet-season conditions; avoid driving at night.
  • Natural hazards: heavy rain, cyclones (e.g., Seroja 2021) and earthquakes are possible; check weather and local advisories.
Pre-departure checklist: (1) latest government/embassy safety & entry advice → (2) vaccinations & insurance → (3) cash (small USD), medicines, insect protection → (4) plan generous travel time for mountains and islands.

6Culture & Travel Etiquette

About 97.6% of people are Catholic, and Sunday Mass and festivals are central to daily life. A distinctive social order endures around the notion of the sacred, lulik.

  • Sacred sites & taboos: do not enter or photograph sacred houses (uma lulik), ritual grounds or restricted objects without permission.
  • Tara bandu: community customary rules on environment and behaviour — follow posted notices and local guidance.
  • Tais: traditional woven cloth (UNESCO intangible heritage, 2021), important in gifts and rituals — treat it with respect.
  • Churches & dress: dress modestly at religious sites and villages; ask before photographing people.
  • Language: a Tetun greeting (e.g., Bondia = good morning) is appreciated.
Did you know? Pope Francis visited in 2024, and about 600,000 people (roughly half the population) gathered for the Mass at Tasi Tolu — faith runs deep here.
