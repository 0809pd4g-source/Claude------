§Executive Summary

  • Restored independence in 2002; became ASEAN's 11th member in 2025. Public finance depends heavily on an ~US$18.8bn Petroleum Fund; diversification beyond oil is the paramount national challenge.
  • Full dollarization removes USD exchange-rate risk, but leaves the country with no independent monetary policy (no rate/currency levers).
  • A young market (population ~1.34m, median age ~21), yet a ~42% poverty rate and a largely informal workforce impose structural limits on purchasing power and skilled labour.
  • Opportunities span agriculture (coffee, tropical crops), ports & logistics (Tibar Bay), the Oecusse special zone (ZEESM), and ASEAN integration.
  • Key risks: the Petroleum Fund depletion outlook (est. 2034–2038), political/security dynamics, governance (CPI 44/100, 73rd), and legal/land tenure complexity.

2Economy — Resource Dependence & Diversification

Most public revenue derives from an oil-and-gas-funded Petroleum Fund, governed by an annual withdrawal rule (ESI = 3% of wealth). Declining production sources and a small non-oil base are the structural constraints.

Key points

  • Production outlook: Bayu-Undan output is winding down; Greater Sunrise development terms remain unsettled. New revenue sources are scarce.
  • Fund depletion: sources vary, but estimates cluster around 2034–2038 (not a fixed figure). Sustainability is the central fiscal issue.
  • Non-oil economy: exports ~US$164m vs imports ~US$909m (2023) — a heavy import dependence; domestic manufacturing is limited.
  • Remittances: the second-largest FX source, driven by managed labour migration to the UK, Korea and Australia.
  • Employment: official unemployment ~1.5% (2023), but with a broad "employment" definition; subsistence farming and informal work dominate. Job creation for a growing young population is urgent.

3Sectors & Opportunities

Agriculture & Food

Coffee (largest region Ermera, organic; the CCT co-op has 20,000+ members). Potential in vanilla and tropical fruit and in tackling the lean season and post-harvest losses.

Ports, Logistics & Infra

Tibar Bay Port (a PPP deep-water port) anchors logistics and trade. Corridors such as National Road No.1 (Dili–Baucau, opened 2022, first JICA yen loan) are advancing.

Special Zone (ZEESM)

The Oecusse special zone (RAEOA) enclave, a stage for logistics, trade and tourism development — though land and delivery challenges warrant scrutiny.

Tourism & Hospitality

Atauro Island's world-class coral reefs and diving; eco/nature tourism. Under-developed tourism infrastructure (lodging, experiences, transport) leaves supply-side room.

ASEAN Integration

Member since 2025. The AEC implementation roadmap 2026–2030 should widen market access and align tariffs/standards over the medium term.

Energy & Power

Electricity access has reached ~100%. Related opportunities in renewables/distributed generation, efficiency, and electrification for a post-oil economy.

Angle: policy favours areas that generate non-oil FX and jobs. Given the small market, export orientation, ASEAN/Australia linkages, and partnering with local co-ops are key.

5Entry & Regulatory Checklist

  • Currency: US dollar (low FX risk vs USD / no independent monetary policy). Accounting and prices are effectively USD-denominated.
  • Entry: Visa on Arrival US$30 / 30 days (extendable). Business residency and licensing are separate — verify.
  • Language: official Tetun and Portuguese; English and Indonesian are also used in practice.
  • ASEAN implications: under the integration roadmap (2026–2030), tariff and standards alignment proceeds in stages.
  • Key institutions: BCTL (central bank), TradeInvest Timor-Leste (investment promotion), World Bank / ADB (development finance), and the watchdog NGO La'o Hamutuk (budget/resource analysis).
  • Due diligence: verify land title, permits and partner bona fides against primary sources and on-the-ground checks. Policies and figures are time-dependent.
Suggested next steps: (1) confirm latest sector regulations/incentives (incl. special zones) from primary sources → (2) engage local bodies (TradeInvest/BCTL) and development finance (ADB/WB) → (3) field due diligence on land, labour and logistics → (4) design market-entry timing around the ASEAN integration schedule.
